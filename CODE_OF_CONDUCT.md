# Code of Conduct

FluxCreate should be a place where people can build, learn, question decisions and share creative work without harassment. This policy applies to contributors, maintainers, users and people representing the project.

## Scope

This policy covers project repositories, issues, pull requests, discussions, project-managed communication channels and events. It also applies when a person explicitly represents FluxCreate in another space. Conduct elsewhere may be considered when it directly affects the safety of participation in the project.

It does not make maintainers responsible for every interaction on the internet. Hosting platforms have their own rules and reporting processes.

## Expected behavior

- Treat people with respect, regardless of experience, identity, background, disability, age, nationality, appearance, religion, gender, sexual orientation or economic circumstances.
- Critique ideas, code and behavior with concrete evidence; avoid attacks on a person's worth or motives.
- Explain assumptions, welcome questions and respect different communication styles.
- Give credit, respect copyright and ask before sharing another person's private work.
- Accept a clear request to stop unwanted contact or discussion.
- Respect maintainers' and contributors' time and stated availability.
- Take responsibility for mistakes, correct misleading claims and make space for repair.
- Use AI assistance responsibly: review what you submit and remain accountable for its content.

Disagreement, a declined feature request, a critical review or an unpopular technical opinion is not by itself a violation.

## Unacceptable behavior

- Harassment, threats, intimidation, stalking, sustained personal attacks or discriminatory abuse.
- Sexual harassment, unwelcome sexual attention or sexualized material directed at participants.
- Publishing or threatening to publish personal information, private messages, credentials or private media without permission.
- Impersonation, deliberate deception about identity or authority, or misuse of privileged project access.
- Repeated unwanted contact, evasion of moderation restrictions or coordinated disruption.
- Deliberately flooding discussions, submitting deceptive reports, or using automation to harass participants.
- Retaliating against someone for raising a concern, participating in a review or declining unwanted contact.

A report made in good faith does not become misconduct because it cannot be substantiated.

## Private reporting

**Email [build@flux-ai.co](mailto:build@flux-ai.co)** with the subject **FluxCreate conduct report**. This is the project reporting mailbox supplied by the project owner. The initial project lead coordinates reports unless conflicted; current roles are listed in [MAINTAINERS.md](MAINTAINERS.md).

Include only what you are comfortable sharing:

- What happened and where, with links or dates if available.
- The people involved and any immediate participation concerns.
- Relevant evidence that you have permission to share.
- A safe way to contact you and any confidentiality or contact restrictions.
- Any requested next step.

You do not need to confront the person involved, suggest a sanction, or compile a complete case before reporting. Do not post sensitive allegations or personal details in a public issue. A report can start with a short request to arrange a safer way to provide evidence.

The mailbox has not been delivery-tested as part of preparing this policy. This is a best-effort reporting route, not an emergency service or a guarantee of response within a fixed time.

Security vulnerabilities and exposed credentials follow [SECURITY.md](.github/SECURITY.md). Ordinary technical help belongs in [SUPPORT.md](SUPPORT.md).

## Conflicts of interest and independent escalation

There is currently **no appointed independent conduct reviewer**. The project mailbox is not an independent or confidential-from-the-project-lead channel.

If a concern involves the project lead, do not send sensitive details to this mailbox unless you are comfortable with that person receiving them. You can request a non-conflicted contact without describing the incident. An independent handler must accept the role, disclose relevant conflicts and obtain your agreement before receiving your report. The project cannot currently guarantee that such a handler will be available.

A person named in a report must not decide that report or an appeal about it. They must not pressure a reporter to withdraw, demand private evidence for personal use or retaliate.

For conduct on GitHub that may violate GitHub's rules, you can use [GitHub's own abuse-reporting process](https://docs.github.com/en/communities/maintaining-your-safety-on-github/reporting-abuse-or-spam). GitHub handles its platform rules; it is not FluxCreate's independent appeals committee.

An independent project escalation contact should be established before a broadly promoted public launch. Do not describe this gap as already resolved.

## Handling reports

The non-conflicted handler should:

1. Acknowledge receipt when able, identify conflicts and discuss immediate participation concerns.
2. Explain who needs access to the report and what next steps are possible.
3. Review available evidence fairly and seek clarification without requiring public disclosure.
4. Give the person whose conduct is at issue an opportunity to respond when safe and appropriate, while minimizing disclosure of the reporter's identity and private information.
5. Choose a proportionate action and explain the decision privately to the people who need it.
6. Record the decision and any restriction, review date or follow-up in a restricted location.

Do not publish raw reports or place them in the source repository. Share identifying details only as necessary for handling the case, safety or applicable obligations. Discuss disclosure limits with the reporter where possible; absolute confidentiality cannot be promised.

Retain only the information needed to handle the report, review restrictions and prevent repeated harm. Restrict access and review whether continued retention is necessary when the case is closed or reviewed. Requests to correct or delete information can be sent to the reporting address and should be considered against these purposes.

## Moderation and consequences

Maintainers may edit, hide or remove violating content and restrict participation in project-controlled spaces. Depending on severity and history, actions can include guidance, a warning, a time-limited interaction restriction, temporary suspension, removal of project privileges or a permanent ban.

Actions need not follow a fixed sequence. A serious threat or deliberate credential disclosure may require immediate containment. Any temporary restriction should state its scope and review point where doing so is safe.

Explain relevant behavior and restrictions without publicly disclosing unnecessary personal details. Do not use conduct enforcement to silence good-faith criticism or technical disagreement.

## Review and appeal

A person affected by a decision can request reconsideration through the reporting address, with the decision concerned, relevant new information or a specific procedural concern. Ask for a non-conflicted handler.

An appeal should be assessed by someone who did not make the original decision and has no relevant conflict. If no such person is available, disclose that limitation; do not describe the original decision-maker's reconsideration as an independent appeal. Temporary protective measures may remain while a review is arranged.

## Policy stewardship

The project lead is responsible for maintaining the reporting route, appointing suitable non-conflicted handlers and updating the roster. Proposed policy changes follow [GOVERNANCE.md](GOVERNANCE.md).

This is a project-specific policy; it does not claim certification by an external conduct organization.
